# Review request — every completed job

## Why this goes to everyone

Google's Maps user-contributed content policy lists among prohibited practices:

> "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive
> reviews from customers"

and

> "merchants should not require or pressure users to leave ratings or write
> reviews while on the premises"

A template sent only to clients who seemed pleased is review gating. It is
also — separately from the policy — the thing that makes a 5.0 look bought. A
profile with a handful of four-star reviews answered well reads as a real
business. A wall of unbroken fives reads as a wall.

So: every completed job, same message, same day. No incentive, no discount, no
"if you're happy". Send it, and take what comes.

## Why Google rather than HomeStars

Not because HomeStars is worth less. Because they do different jobs and one of
them is already done.

- **HomeStars** is a destination: someone already looking for a contractor goes
  there and compares. That profile exists and is linked from the site's
  `sameAs`, which is how Google knows it is us.
- **Google** is the surface. Google Business Profile ratings are what produce
  the stars in local search results and in the map pack, and Google reads them
  from its own data — they cannot be marked up on the website, at all, by
  anyone. They are also the reputation signal that AI answer engines read back
  when someone asks for a recommendation.

The site cannot manufacture that. Only completed jobs can.

## Timing

Send when the job is genuinely finished and the client has walked the floor —
not on the last day of work, when the crew is still packing. For a refinish that
is typically the day after the final coat is walkable; for an install, after the
walkthrough.

## Setup checklist

1. **Get the short review link.** Google Business Profile → Read reviews → Get
   more reviews. It looks like `g.page/r/…/review`. Use that exact link, not a
   search URL — every extra click loses people.
2. **Claim and verify the Business Profile first**, if that has not happened.
   Nothing above works without it, and the profile is also what makes the
   business eligible for the map pack at all.
3. **Check the NAP matches the site exactly** — name, address, phone. The site
   publishes `Ecowoods Inc.`, `32 Norfield Crescent, Toronto, ON M9W 1X6`,
   `(647) 244-5156`, from `BUSINESS_NAP`. A profile that says Etobicoke where
   the site says Toronto is a mismatch to a matching algorithm, however obvious
   it looks to a person. Pick one and make everything agree with it.
4. **Answer every review**, including — especially — the critical ones. A
   specific, non-defensive reply to a three-star review is more persuasive to
   the next reader than the five above it.
5. **Never offer anything for a review.** Not a discount, not a draw, not a gift
   card. It is explicitly prohibited, and it is detectable.
