**Subject:** A heritage floor can be sanded a fixed number of times. Most owners
find out afterwards.

Hi [Name],

There is a number nobody tells homeowners with a hundred-year-old floor: how
many times it can still be sanded.

Every full sand removes material. The wear layer above the tongue is finite, and
once it is gone the floor is not restorable at any price — it is replaceable.
A contractor who opens with 36 grit on a floor that needed a screen and recoat
has spent a decade of that floor's remaining life in an afternoon, and the
result looks *better* on handover day. That is what makes it hard to see.

The distinction is not subtle to anyone in the trade, and it is completely
invisible to the person paying:

- **Screen and recoat** abrades the finish only. The wood is untouched.
- **Full sand** goes to bare wood, and costs material that does not come back.
