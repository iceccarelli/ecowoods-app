Beautiful space, but the concrete was poured directly on soil with no depressurization system. A calcium chloride test revealed the truth:\n\n**9.8 MVTR** — nearly double the 5.0 safe threshold for hardwood.\n\nWhat does that mean?\n\n**In 30 days, the wood would reach 13–15% moisture content.** Cupping would become visible 3–4 weeks post-installation. The finish would fail. Property damage.\n\nThis is why basements are hard. The concrete itself is a wick for groundwater moisture. Standard surface vapor barriers (polyethylene sheet) fail after 8–12 weeks because hydrostatic pressure from below forces moisture through micro-perforations.\n\nThe owner's first contractor said, *\"We'll just use a really good vapor barrier and seal everything tight.\"* That approach fails 100% of the time on high-moisture basements. The pressure wins.\n\n## The Solution: Sub-Slab Depressurization\n\nThe only reliable solution for 9+ MVTR is **sub-slab depressurization**—a radon mitigation specialist installs a PVC collection network 4 feet below the concrete, creating suction that pulls moisture vapor away from the slab before it reaches the surface.\n\n### What the System Does\n\n1. **PVC collection pipes installed below concrete slab**\n   - Runs the perimeter and center of the basement\n   - Tapped into concrete via entry points\n\n2. **Suction point (like a reverse chimney)**\n   - Creates 5–10 Pa negative pressure below the slab\n   - Soil moisture migrates down into collection pipes, not up through concrete\n\n3. **Rooftop exhaust**\n   - Moisture vented outside, above the roofline\n   - Zero chance of re-intrusion\n\n### The Results\n\n**Before:** 9.8 MVTR (critical, unsafe for wood)\n**After:** 0.6 MVTR (99% reduction, excellent)\n\nThis single engineering decision made the rest of the project possible.\n\n## Phase 2: Humidity Control (Year-Round RH Stabilization)\n\nEven with sub-slab depressurization, the basement had a second problem: **seasonal humidity swings.**\n\n- **Winter (heating on):** RH dropped to 35–40% (too dry)\n- **Spring/Fall (transitional):** Outside humidity (75–85%) overwhelmed the HVAC return → RH spiked to 65–70%\n- **Summer:** RH oscillated 40+ percentage points\n\nWood cannot acclimate to a moving target. With ±20% RH swings, the wood expands/contracts continuously. Finish adhesion degrades. Movement becomes visible.\n\n**Solution:** Dedicated dehumidifier + RH sensor\n\n- Added 50-pint/day Energy Star dehumidifier\n- Connected to existing HVAC return (integrated with main system)\n- RH sensor set to 45–55% target\n- If RH exceeds 58%, dehumidifier activates\n- If RH drops below 42%, humidifier pad activates on main HVAC\n- **Result:** RH stable ±3% year-round (massive improvement)\n\nThis is as important as the vapor barrier. Many contractors install the barrier and forget humidity control. The moisture migrates through air, not just through concrete. You need both.\n\n## Phase 3: Extended Wood Acclimation (96 Hours)\n\nNormal acclimation is 48 hours. For this basement, we extended it to **96 hours** because:\n\n1.
