# Species Comparison Matrix — Toronto Renovations (Janka, Cost, Tannin Risk, Use Cases)

Choosing a hardwood species is not a cosmetic decision. It's an engineering decision with cost, risk, and timeline implications.

White oak and red oak dominate Toronto's market (70%+ of installs), but they're not interchangeable. Add maple, ash, cherry, walnut, and hickory—each with wildly different density, tannin chemistry, moisture response, and installation burden—and the decision space becomes complex.

This guide provides species specifications, failure modes, and decision frameworks so you match species to project requirements instead of guessing.

## Detailed Species Profiles

### 1. White Oak — The Premium Challenger

**Key Stats:**
- Janka: 1,360
- Cost: $6–12/sqft (raw material; finished = $8–18)
- Tannin: PYROGALLOL (soluble, water-reactive, iron-reactive)
- Density: 0.75 (dense; tight grain)
- Grain: Prominent ray fleck (medullary rays visible), coarse texture
- Color: Light tan to golden, darkens with UV
- Smell: Strong, musty during first 2–4 weeks

**Installation Burden:**

- **Acclimation:** 14–21 days (slower to dry than red oak; denser wood)
- **MVTR threshold:** Install only if subfloor MVTR <5 (vapor barrier mandatory if >5)
- **RH window:** 55% max at install (tannin staining risk accelerates above 60% RH)
- **Fasteners:** Stainless steel ONLY (galvanized iron → black stains around nails)
- **Finishing:** Acrylic poly or oil-based ONLY (water-based = tannin leaching risk unless formulated for white oak)

**Failure Mode 1: Tannin Staining**

```
Scenario: July installation, 65% RH, water-based poly
  ↓
Moisture + white oak tannins (soluble) → tannate solution
  ↓
Tannate + iron (fastener, dust, water hardness) → BLACK COMPLEX
  ↓
3–6 weeks later: Blotchy dark spots, especially around fasteners
  ↓
Permanent. Requires full re-sand + refinish ($6–10k per 1,000 sqft)
```

**Failure Mode 2: Over-Drying in Winter**

```
Scenario: Acclimate in October (40% RH) to 7.5% MC, install Nov
  ↓
Customer's heating system cranks in December (30% RH)
  ↓
Wood shrinks. Gaps open (1/8"+ between boards)
  ↓
Customer sees cracks, calls angry
```

**Why Choose White Oak:**
- Aesthetics (ray fleck is signature)
- Durability (1,360 Janka is solid)
- Stability IF done right (proof: Distillery District case study)
- Premium pricing justifies installation complexity

**Why Avoid White Oak:**
- High tannin risk in summer or humid basements
- Difficult acclimation (21 days vs 7 for ash)
- Stainless fastener cost (+$500–1,000 per job)
- Requires species-specific finish knowledge

**Contractor Recommendation:** Limit white oak to dry season (Nov–Mar), main floors only, high-end projects where you can charge premium for complexity.

### 2. Red Oak — The Reliable Workhorse

**Key Stats:**
- Janka: 1,290 (nearly white oak, slightly softer)
- Cost: $5–9/sqft (raw; finished = $7–14)
- Tannin: CONDENSED (insoluble, stable, much less reactive)
- Density: 0.74 (slightly less dense than white oak)
- Grain: Open pores (visible on finish), less formal appearance
- Color: Tan to reddish-brown, slightly warmer than white oak
- Smell: Musty (similar to white oak, slightly less pronounced)

**Installation Burden:**

- **Acclimation:** 7–14 days (dries faster than white oak)
- **MVTR threshold:** Install if subfloor MVTR <8 (barrier recommended if >5, not mandatory)
- **RH window:** 60% max at install (more tolerant than white oak)
- **Fasteners:** Galvanized acceptable (stainless preferred, but galvanized works)
- **Finishing:** Standard water-based poly acceptable (risk much lower than white oak)

**Why Choose Red Oak:**
- Cost (~25% cheaper than white oak, same Janka)
- Faster acclimation (vs white oak)
- Lower tannin risk (allows water-based finishes)
- Familiar (most contractors know red oak)
- Warm color (some prefer to white oak's cool tone)

**Why Avoid Red Oak:**
- Less distinctive aesthetically (open pores, less ray fleck)
- Perceived as "standard" (commoditized)
- Price pressure (customers expect discounts vs premium species)

**Toronto Market Reality:** Red oak is 50%+ of residential installs. It's the baseline. Customers choose it when budget-conscious or when white oak complexity isn't needed.

**Contractor Recommendation:** Position red oak as "proven, reliable, cost-effective." Use for multi-unit projects, rentals, where ROI matters more than luxury.

### 3. Hard Maple — The Tank

**Key Stats:**
- Janka: 1,450 (hardest common species)
- Cost: $7–14/sqft (raw; finished = $10–18)
- Tannin: None (non-reactive)
- Density: 0.75 (dense; fine grain; tight)
- Grain: Subtle, understated (appears almost uniform)
- Color: Pale cream to white, minimal variation, very uniform
- Smell: Minimal odor

**Installation Burden:**

- **Acclimation:** 10–14 days (dense wood, slow to equilibrate)
- **MVTR threshold:** Install if subfloor <8 MVTR (maple is stable; barrier optional if <5)
- **RH window:** 65% (most tolerant of all; not moisture-sensitive)
- **Fasteners:** Any (stainless or galvanized; no tannin risk)
- **Finishing:** Any (oil-based, water-based, acrylic poly all work; no chemistry issues)

**Why Choose Hard Maple:**
- Hardness (1,450 = ultra-durable, can take abuse)
- Zero tannin issues (chemistry-proof)
- Very stable (low MC swing = minimal gaps)
- Neutral color (matches any décor)
- Moisture-forgiving (no MVTR obsession)

**Why Avoid Hard Maple:**
- Cost (~50% premium vs red oak)
- Bland aesthetics (some find uniform boring vs character)
- Sanding difficulty (dense = dust, heat, tool wear)
- Acclimation is slow (dense wood takes time)

**Best Use Cases:**
- Commercial (kitchens, high traffic)
- Schools, gyms (basketball courts)
- Kitchens in wet environments
- Customers who want zero maintenance worry

**Contractor Recommendation:** Upsell hard maple in kitchens ("won't show wear") and for high-traffic areas. Position durability over beauty.

### 4. Ash — The Contemporary Choice

**Key Stats:**
- Janka: 1,320 (hard, competitive with white oak)
- Cost: $4–8/sqft (raw; finished = $6–12)
- Tannin: Very low (condensed, like red oak)
- Density: 0.64 (lighter than oak; less dense)
- Grain: Open pores, pronounced grain pattern (similar to red oak)
- Color: Pale cream to light brown, excellent staining acceptance
- Smell: Minimal

**Installation Burden:**

- **Acclimation:** 7–10 days (fast; less dense than oak)
- **MVTR threshold:** Install if subfloor <8 MVTR
- **RH window:** 60% (tolerant)
- **Fasteners:** Galvanized acceptable
- **Finishing:** Any; excellent stain adhesion

**Why Choose Ash:**
- Cost (lowest of hard species; $4–8)
- Hardness (1,320 = solid durability)
- Fast acclimation (7–10 days; quick to install)
- Grain accepts stain beautifully (can customize color)
- Low tannin (no chemistry issues)

**Why Avoid Ash:**
- Less distinctive than oak (modern look, not classic)
- Open pores require more finish (sealed finish necessary)
- Lighter color (some homeowners want darker tones)

**Toronto Market Reality:** Ash is gaining traction in contemporary/modern homes. Younger buyers prefer it to traditional oak.

**Contractor Recommendation:** Recommend ash for cost-conscious customers in modern homes. Upsell with "accepts custom staining."

### 5. Hickory — The Rustic Statement

**Key Stats:**
- Janka: 1,820 (extremely hard; second only to Brazilian Walnut)
- Cost: $8–15/sqft (raw; finished = $12–20)
- Tannin: Very low
- Density: 0.75 (dense; dense wood)
- Grain: Highly variable (character, color variation)
- Color: Mix of cream, tan, brown, reddish (intentional variation)
- Smell: Minimal

**Installation Burden:**

- **Acclimation:** 10–14 days (dense)
- **MVTR threshold:** <8 MVTR
- **RH window:** 65% (stable)
- **Fasteners:** Any
- **Finishing:** Any

**Why Choose Hickory:**
- Durability (1,820 Janka = nearly indestructible)
- Character (color variation = high-end, rustic aesthetic)
- Stability (dense, forgiving)

**Why Avoid Hickory:**
- Cost (premium)
- Character variation (some homeowners want uniformity, not rustic)
- Niche market (not mass-market appeal)

**Best Use Cases:**
- High-end rustic/farmhouse homes
- Lakeside cottages
- Character-driven renovations
- Customers who value durability + aesthetics

## Decision Framework: Which Species for Your Project?

### Decision Tree 1: Budget-First

```
Budget constraint: <$8/sqft total cost?
  ├─ YES → Ash ($4–8), Engineered ($4–7), Bamboo ($3–7)
  │        ⚠️ Trade-off: Lower durability or engineered core
  └─ NO → Continue to aesthetics
         
Aesthetics: Want character or modern look?
  ├─ Character (grain, color variation) → Hickory, Red Oak, Walnut
  └─ Modern (uniform, clean) → Maple, Cherry, Ash

Installation timeline: Can acclimate 14+ days?
  ├─ NO → Choose fastest species (Ash 7d, Cherry 5d)
  └─ YES → Any species works; choose by aesthetics

Installation location: Main floor or basement?
  ├─ Basement, high MVTR → Engineered, avoid white oak
  └─ Main floor → Any species works
```

### Decision Tree 2: Aesthetics-First

```
Desired appearance?
  ├─ Warm, golden, formal → White Oak
  ├─ Warm, open-grain, classic → Red Oak
  ├─ Pale, modern, clean → Maple, Cherry, Ash
  ├─ Dark, upscale → Walnut
  └─ Rustic, character → Hickory

Acceptable risk: Willing to manage white oak tannin complexity?
  ├─ NO → Choose red oak, ash, maple, cherry, walnut, hickory
  └─ YES → White oak is choice for premium aesthetic

Timeline: When install?
  ├─ June–August (humid) → Avoid white oak; choose maple, cherry, walnut, ash
  └─ Nov–Mar (dry) → White oak acceptable
```

### Decision Tree 3: Risk-Minimization

```
Zero tolerance for callbacks?
  ├─ High-traffic, wet kitchen → Hard Maple (1,450 Janka, no chemistry issues)
  ├─ Main living area → Red Oak, Ash (proven, forgiving)
  ├─ Basement → Engineered or Maple (stable, moisture-tolerant)
  └─ Luxury/showcase → White Oak (IF Nov–Mar, IF you manage complexity)

Moisture environment: High humidity or wet?
  ├─ YES → Maple, Cherry, Walnut, Engineered (skip white oak)
  └─ NO → Any species

Customer sophistication: Will they understand maintenance?
  ├─ NO → Red Oak, Ash, Maple (low-maintenance, forgiving)
  └─ YES → White Oak acceptable (requires seasonal humidity management)
```

## Janka Hardness & Real-World Wear

**Janka score** measures hardness (resistance to denting/scratching). Higher = more resistant.

| Janka Range | Interpretation | Real-World Implication |
|---|---|---|
| <1,000 | Soft | Cherry, Walnut, Chestnut; shows foot traffic wear within 5 years |
| 1,000–1,300 | Moderate | Red Oak, Ash; shows wear, but acceptable for residential |
| 1,300–1,500 | Hard | White Oak, Maple, Hickory; resists wear well, good for kitchens |
| 1,500+ | Very Hard | Pecan, Brazilian Walnut; commercial-grade durability |

**Reality Check:** A 1,450 Janka (hard maple) will look pristine 10 years in a busy kitchen. A 995 Janka (cherry) will show scratches after 5 years in the same kitchen. Both are beautiful; both are "hardwood." The difference is wear visibility, not structural failure.

## Tannin Risk Matrix

| Species | Tannin Type | Solubility | Water Sensitivity | Iron Reactivity | Risk in >60% RH |
|---------|---|---|---|---|---|
| White Oak | Pyrogallol | HIGH | Very High | EXTREMELY HIGH | Very High (70%+ staining risk) |
| Red Oak | Condensed | Low | Low | Low | Low (5% staining risk) |
| Ash | Condensed | Low | Low | Low | Very Low |
| Cherry | None | N/A | Moderate | None | None (darkens with UV, not tannin) |
| Walnut | None | N/A | Moderate | None | None |
| Maple | None | N/A | Moderate | None | None |
| Hickory | None | N/A | Moderate | None | None |

**Summary:** White oak is the only species where tannin chemistry is a real risk. All others are chemistry-safe. If tannin concerns exist, recommend red oak (similar look to white oak, zero tannin risk).

## Installation Difficulty Ranking

| Difficulty | Species | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Very Easy** | Cherry, Walnut, Chestnut | Low density, fast acclimation, no chemistry issues |
| **Easy** | Ash, Red Oak, Engineered | Proven, forgiving, common |
| **Moderate** | Maple, Hickory, Pecan | Dense (slow acclimation), but no chemistry issues |
| **Difficult** | White Oak | Dense + tannin chemistry + fastener sensitivity |
| **Very Difficult** | Brazilian Walnut | Extremely dense (sanding nightmare), very expensive (high stakes) |

## Summary

**Choose species strategically:**

- **Budget-driven?** Ash or Red Oak ($4–9/sqft)
- **Durability-driven?** Hard Maple or Hickory (1,450+ Janka)
- **Aesthetics-driven?** White Oak (formal) or Walnut (dark luxury)
- **Risk-minimized?** Red Oak or Ash (proven, forgiving)
- **Summer installation?** Avoid white oak; choose maple, cherry, ash, walnut
- **Wet environment (kitchen, bathroom)?** Hard Maple or Engineered (skip white oak)

Each species is not "better." Each species is **fit for purpose**. Match species to project constraints, customer expectations, and seasonal timing. When you do, callbacks disappear and reputations build.

**EcoWoods approach:** We interview the customer, assess moisture conditions, understand timeline, and recommend species accordingly. Premium customers get white oak (with full tannin protocols). Cost-conscious customers get ash or red oak. Everyone gets exactly what their project needs—and everyone is happy.
