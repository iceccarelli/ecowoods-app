# Wood Acclimation Timeline for Toronto / GTA — Seasonal Guides & Failure Modes

Wood is hygroscopic. It absorbs and releases moisture to match its environment. In Toronto, where relative humidity (RH) swings 30–70% over a calendar year, that equilibrium is a moving target. Install wood at 10% MC when the environment is 50% RH, and within two months, as RH drops to 35%, that wood will shrink—cupping, crowning, finish failure, callbacks.

This guide provides exact acclimation timelines for Toronto's four seasons, species-specific protocols, failure modes to watch for, and decision trees to avoid the most expensive mistakes.

## Why Acclimation Matters in Toronto

Toronto's humidity profile is more extreme than most North American cities:

- **Winter (Dec–Feb):** Heated homes: 20–35% RH. Outdoor: 40–60% RH. Swing: 25–40 percentage points.
- **Spring (Mar–May):** Rapid transitions. March: 35% RH. May: 60% RH. Change rate: ~1% RH per day.
- **Summer (Jun–Aug):** High RH, high MC risk. Peak: 60–70% RH, often 75%+ in basements.
- **Fall (Sep–Nov):** Cooling and drying. October: 50% RH. November: 35% RH. Reverse swift.

**The acclimation imperative:** Wood must match the RH it will experience _after installation_, not the RH at delivery. If you acclimate to 50% RH in March (spring delivery), but the customer will live through summer humidity at 70% RH, the wood will re-absorb moisture after install, causing cupping.

**Toronto baseline targets:**

| Season | Typical Indoor RH | Target Wood MC | Acclimation Days |
|--------|---|---|---|
| January–February | 25–32% | 6–8% | 3–5 |
| March–April | 35–50% | 8–10% | 7–14 |
| May–June | 50–62% | 10–12% | 14–21 |
| July–August | 55–70% | 11–13% | 21–28 |
| September–October | 40–55% | 8–10% | 7–14 |
| November–December | 28–40% | 7–9% | 5–10 |

## Understanding Equilibrium Moisture Content (EMC)

**EMC** is the theoretical moisture content at which wood stops exchanging moisture with its environment.

**Formula (Hailwood-Horrobin):**

```
EMC = (1.944 × K × RH%) / (1 − K × RH% + K² × RH%²)

where K = 0.325 at 70°F to 0.485 at 68°F (typical Toronto indoor range)
```

**Practical interpretation (no math needed):**

| RH % | EMC (%) | Dryness |
|---|---|---|
| 25% | 4.5–5.5% | Very dry (winter, heated home) |
| 35% | 6.5–7.5% | Dry (winter, normal) |
| 50% | 9.0–10.0% | Moderate (spring/fall baseline) |
| 60% | 11.0–12.0% | Moist (summer, humid) |
| 70% | 13.0–14.5% | Very moist (basements, wet summer) |
| 80% | 15.5–17.0% | Problem zone (risk of mold, tannin leaching) |

**Rule of thumb:** Wood will naturally trend toward 1 percentage point below EMC. If the environment is 50% RH (EMC 9.5%), installed wood will stabilize at ~8.5% MC.

## Winter Acclimation: January–February (Best Season)

### The Setup

**Outdoor conditions:**
- RH: 40–60% (dry, frozen air)
- Temperature: -5 to +5°C (below freezing typically)

**Indoor conditions:**
- RH: 20–32% (heated home, furnace running)
- Temperature: 21–23°C
- Vapor pressure differential: Extreme (outdoor air is dry)

**Window:** Ideal for installation. Lowest RH of the year means wood will dry aggressively after install; you want pre-acclimation to target that final state.

### Acclimation Protocol: Winter

**Goal:** Acclimate wood to 6–8% MC (matches 25–32% RH winter environment)

**Timeline:**

1. **Pre-delivery assessment** (Day 0):
   - Customer home: Test RH in 5 locations (avg = baseline)
   - If baseline RH >35%, consider heat + dehumidifier for 48–72 hours before delivery
   - Typical winter baseline: 28–32% RH
   - Subfloor MC target: <8% (verify with calcium chloride test)

2. **Wood delivery + acclimation** (Days 1–5):
   - Open flooring boxes, spread planks loosely in installation area
   - Leave space for air circulation (1 inch between plank piles)
   - Do NOT acclimate in a damp basement or unfinished room (RH may spike)
   - Monitor: Take moisture meter readings daily on 5 sample planks
   - Target trajectory:
     - Day 1: 9–11% MC (as-delivered from warehouse at ~10% RH)
     - Day 3: 8–9% MC (drying trend)
     - Day 5: 7–8% MC (stable)

3. **Installation window** (Days 5–7):
   - Install when wood MC = 7–8% (within 0.5% of subfloor MC)
   - Install when home RH = 25–35%
   - Proceed with fastening, finishing, curing

4. **Post-install monitoring** (Weeks 1–4):
   - Maintain home RH at 25–35% (furnace dehumidifies naturally)
   - Monitor no gaps opening (width >1/8" = failure sign)
   - Monitor finish cure (polyurethane cures fastest in dry conditions)
   - By Week 4: Gaps should be <1/16" (normal seasonal movement)

### Winter Failure Mode: Pre-Curing Moisture Rebound

**Risk:** Wood delivered too wet (>10% MC in December) sits for 3–5 days and doesn't dry enough before install.

**Cause:** Winter outdoor RH may rise to 50–60% if rain/snow preceding delivery day; warehouse humidity creeps up.

**Prevention:**
- Verify as-delivered MC with meter before accepting shipment
- If >10% MC, extend acclimation to 7–10 days
- Use dehumidifier in acclimation area if home RH >35%

**Failure signature:** 4–8 weeks post-install, uniform gaps appear (wood swelled after install as RH spiked). Finish adhesion intact, but gaps are visible.

## Spring Acclimation: March–May (Moderate Difficulty)

### The Setup

**Outdoor conditions:**
- March: RH 40–50%, temp 0–10°C
- April: RH 45–55%, temp 5–15°C
- May: RH 50–62%, temp 10–20°C

**Indoor conditions:**
- Heat still running, but windows opening on warm days
- RH: 35–50% early spring, trending to 55%+ by May
- Risk: Uneven acclimation if HVAC is inconsistent

**Window:** Moderate risk. RH is rising through spring, so wood acclimated in March may be under-acclimated by May. Verify installation date aligns with acclimation RH target.

### Acclimation Protocol: Spring

**Goal:** Acclimate wood to 8–10% MC (matches 35–50% RH spring environment)

**Key decision:** Is installation happening in March, April, or May?

#### March Installation

**Timeline (7–10 days):**

1. **Pre-delivery:** Test home RH (target 35–40%)
2. **Delivery + acclimation (Days 1–7):**
   - Open boxes, spread planks
   - Daily meter readings
   - Trajectory: 10–11% MC (as-delivered) → 8–9% MC (Day 7)
3. **Install (Day 7–8):** When wood MC = 8–9%, home RH = 35–40%
4. **Post-install:** Monitor humidity for April spike (expect 5–10% RH increase)

#### April Installation

**Timeline (10–14 days):**

1. **Pre-delivery:** Test home RH (target 40–48%)
   - April humidity is rising; acclimate for the mid-month RH, not the day-of RH
2. **Delivery + acclimation (Days 1–10):**
   - Same process, but expect slower drying (higher ambient RH)
   - Trajectory: 10–11% → 8.5–9.5% MC (Day 10)
3. **Install (Day 10–12):** When wood MC = 8.5–9.5%, home RH = 40–48%

#### May Installation

**Timeline (14–21 days):**

1. **Pre-delivery:** Test home RH (target 50–55%)
   - May is humid; expect acclimation to stall at ~10–11% MC if RH >55%
   - If home RH already >55%, consider delaying to June (or summer protocol)
2. **Delivery + acclimation (Days 1–14):**
   - Expect slower drying
   - May need dehumidifier to pull RH down to 50–52%
   - Trajectory: 10–11% → 9.5–10.5% MC (Day 14)
3. **Install (Day 14–16):** When wood MC = 9.5–10.5%, home RH = 50–55%

### Spring Failure Mode: Rapid RH Changes

**Risk:** Acclimate in early April (RH 40%) for week, then install mid-April (RH 50% suddenly).

**Cause:** Unseasonal weather (warm day, windows opened) or HVAC changes cause RH spike between acclimation finish and install.

**Prevention:**
- Use 5-day buffer. Acclimate to RH target, then verify RH again 2 days before install.
- If RH changed >5%, extend acclimation 3–5 more days.
- Install date is not fixed; environmental conditions are. Move install if RH drifts.

**Failure signature:** Installation goes well, but 2–4 weeks later, as May humidity settles in, wood begins absorbing moisture. Finish adhesion stresses. Micro-checking appears (fine cracks in finish).

## Summer Acclimation: June–August (High Risk, Longest Timeline)

### The Setup

**Outdoor conditions:**
- RH: 60–70% (very humid)
- Temperature: 20–28°C
- Absolute moisture: High (air is saturated)

**Indoor conditions (with HVAC):**
- RH: 55–65% (air conditioning helps, but infiltration is challenge)
- Temperature: 21–23°C
- Basement RH: Often 65–75% (ground moisture, limited AC reach)

**Window:** Most difficult season. High acclimation burden. Longest timeline. Highest risk of tannin staining (moisture triggers tannin leaching in oak).

### Acclimation Protocol: Summer

**Goal:** Acclimate wood to 11–13% MC (matches 55–70% RH summer environment)

**Critical decision:** Is the wood going into an air-conditioned space or an uncontrolled basement?

#### AC-Controlled Space (Main Floor)

**Timeline (14–21 days):**

1. **Pre-delivery (Days -3 to 0):**
   - Test AC + humidity. Set thermostat to 22°C, run continuously for 72 hours.
   - Measure RH in 5 locations (avg should be 50–60%)
   - If RH >65%, increase AC capacity or use supplemental dehumidifier

2. **Delivery + acclimation (Days 1–14):**
   - Open boxes immediately (day 1)
   - Spread planks loosely (1-inch gaps between piles)
   - Run AC + dehumidifier continuously
   - Target RH during acclimation: 50–55% (pull down from 65% if needed)
   - Daily meter readings on 5+ samples
   - Expected trajectory: 10–12% MC (as-delivered) → 11–12% MC (Day 14)
     - Note: Wood may NOT dry much; goal is stabilization, not drying
   - Verify subfloor MC is 10–11% (if >12%, delay install)

3. **Install (Day 14–16):**
   - When wood MC = 11–12%, home RH = 50–55%
   - Finish (water-based or acrylic poly) in stable RH environment
   - Maintain AC during cure (48–72 hours)

4. **Post-install (Weeks 1–4):**
   - Maintain RH 50–60% continuously (AC must stay on during heat wave)
   - Monitor finish adhesion (summer high humidity + water-based finish = risk)
   - By Week 4: Expect minimal gaps; wood is at equilibrium

#### Basement (Uncontrolled RH, High MVTR)

**Timeline (21–28 days):**

1. **Pre-delivery (Days -7 to 0):**
   - MVTR test (calcium chloride)
   - If MVTR >5, vapor barrier is mandatory
   - Measure basement RH: Typical 60–75% (expect equilibrium at 12–14% MC)
   - Install dehumidifier (target 55–60% RH) BEFORE wood arrives
   - Run dehumidifier for 72 hours pre-delivery to acclimate the space

2. **Delivery + acclimation (Days 1–21):**
   - Open boxes immediately (critical to avoid condensation inside)
   - Spread planks (high air circulation vital)
   - Run dehumidifier continuously (24/7)
   - Daily meter readings on 8+ samples (sample from multiple pile heights)
   - Target RH: 55–60% (dehumidifier pulls basement down from 70–75%)
   - Expected trajectory: 11–13% MC (as-delivered) → 11–12% MC (Day 21)
     - Drying is slow in high-moisture environment; goal is stabilization + dehumidifier strategy proof
   - Verify subfloor moisture barrier is installed and cured (48–72 hours)

3. **Install (Day 21–24):**
   - When wood MC = 11–12%, basement RH = 55–60% (with dehumidifier running)
   - Install moisture barrier + wood
   - Use acrylic poly or oil-based finish (NOT water-based; vapor pressure is high)
   - Dehumidifier stays on during cure (48–72 hours)

4. **Post-install (Weeks 1–8):**
   - Dehumidifier strategy is now permanent (customer keeps running continuously)
   - Baseline: Maintain RH 50–60% year-round (summer + winter)
   - Month 1: Monitor for cupping (= barrier failure or humidity spike)
   - Month 2–3: Finish should be stable; verify no adhesion loss

### Summer Failure Mode 1: Insufficient Acclimation Timeline

**Risk:** Contractor delivers wood Friday, installs Tuesday (4 days). Wood still at 12–13% MC, environment is 70% RH.

**Cause:** Summer deadlines. Customer wants install ASAP. Contractor underestimates acclimation burden.

**Prevention:**
- Contract specifies minimum 14–21 day summer acclimation (not negotiable)
- If customer demands faster: Offer temporary dehumidifier rental ($50/day) to accelerate RH pulldown
- Document RH acclimation in writing (email to customer: "Acclimation began July 1, earliest install is July 15")

**Failure signature:** 3–6 weeks post-install (August), as outdoor RH stabilizes and AC loses battle, indoor RH creeps to 65%+. Wood absorbs moisture. Cupping begins. Finish micro-checks. Customer blames installer.

### Summer Failure Mode 2: Basement Dehumidifier Not Maintained

**Risk:** Basement floor installed with dehumidifier running. Homeowner turns off dehumidifier in October (thinks it's done). Winter heating dries home to 30% RH, but basement RH rebounds to 65% in summer.

**Cause:** Lack of customer education. No written protocol for year-round dehumidifier use.

**Prevention:**
- Install warranty includes: "Dehumidifier must be operated continuously. Failure to maintain creates warranty void."
- Provide written schedule: "Winter: maintain 30–40% RH. Summer: maintain 50–60% RH."
- Recommend smart humidistat (auto on/off based on RH setpoint)
- Follow-up email at season transitions (Nov 1 + June 1)

**Failure signature:** June of year 2, customer notices cupping. Calls contractor (30 months later). Fight over warranty, responsibility, cost. Contractor pays for replacement ($6k–10k).

## Fall Acclimation: September–October (Moderate Difficulty)

### The Setup

**Outdoor conditions:**
- September: RH 50–60%, cooling (not yet cold)
- October: RH 40–55%, drying (heating season begins)

**Indoor conditions:**
- HVAC shifts from AC to heat; thermostat changes
- RH tends to drop rapidly (heating dries air)
- Risk: Acclimate in September (50% RH), install in October (35% RH), wood over-dried

**Window:** Good window, but transition risk. Timing matters.

### Acclimation Protocol: Fall

**Goal:** Acclimate wood to 8–10% MC (matches 40–55% RH fall environment)

#### September Installation

**Timeline (10–14 days):**

1. **Pre-delivery:** Test home RH (target 50–55%)
2. **Delivery + acclimation (Days 1–10):**
   - Open boxes, spread planks
   - Daily meter readings
   - Trajectory: 10–12% MC (as-delivered) → 8.5–9.5% MC (Day 10)
3. **Install (Day 10–12):** When wood MC = 8.5–9.5%, home RH = 50–55%

#### October Installation

**Timeline (7–10 days):**

1. **Pre-delivery (Days -3 to 0):**
   - Turn OFF air conditioning completely
   - Let home RH rise to 45–50% (takes 48 hours typically; outside RH is 50–55%)
   - Verify thermostat will stay OFF during acclimation (don't let heating system fire up)
   - Test home RH: If already 40%, this is fine; proceed with standard protocol

2. **Delivery + acclimation (Days 1–7):**
   - Open boxes, spread planks
   - Daily meter readings
   - Trajectory: 10–12% MC → 7.5–8.5% MC (Day 7)
   - If home RH drops below 40% (early heating), extend acclimation to Day 10

3. **Install (Day 7–9):** When wood MC = 7.5–8.5%, home RH = 40–50%

### Fall Failure Mode: Heating System Dry-Out

**Risk:** Customer's heating system kicks on during acclimation. RH drops from 50% to 35% in 48 hours. Wood over-dries.

**Cause:** Early October cold snap. Thermostat set to 20°C. House temperature drops overnight, heat fires up.

**Prevention:**
- Schedule acclimation for late September (before heating) or early November (after heat ramp-up settles)
- Instruct customer: "Turn thermostat to OFF during acclimation week" (via email + phone)
- Monitor home RH daily; if it drops >10%, extend acclimation
- Have contingency: If RH crashes, apply water bottle humidification to raise RH back to target

**Failure signature:** Install goes well, but 4–8 weeks later (December, full heating season), wood shrinks. Gaps open (1/8" or wider). Finish adhesion stresses.

## Species-Specific Acclimation Adjustments

Not all wood acclimate at the same rate. Denser species (red oak, maple) acclimate slower. Lighter species (ash, cherry) acclimate faster.

| Species | Density | Acclimation Rate | Adjust Timeline |
|---------|---------|---|---|
| White Oak | Dense (0.75) | Slowest | +3–5 days |
| Red Oak | Dense (0.74) | Slowest | +3–5 days |
| Hard Maple | Very dense (0.75) | Slowest | +5–7 days |
| Ash | Medium (0.64) | Moderate | No adjustment |
| Cherry | Medium (0.58) | Moderate | No adjustment |
| Walnut | Medium (0.58) | Moderate | No adjustment |
| White Ash | Medium (0.64) | Moderate | No adjustment |
| Hickory | Very dense (0.75) | Slowest | +5–7 days |

**Rule:** Add 3–5 days for oak/maple/hickory; use standard timeline for others.

## Quick Reference: Acclimation Decision Tree

```
START: Wood delivery date scheduled
  ↓
Question 1: What month?
  ├─ Jan–Feb → WINTER protocol (3–5 days)
  ├─ Mar–Apr → SPRING protocol (7–14 days)
  ├─ May–Jun → HIGH RH protocol (14–21 days)
  ├─ Jul–Aug → SUMMER protocol (21–28 days) 
  ├─ Sep → FALL-EARLY protocol (10–14 days)
  └─ Oct–Nov → FALL-LATE protocol (7–10 days)
  ↓
Question 2: Species?
  ├─ Oak / Maple / Hickory → ADD 3–5 days
  └─ Other → No adjustment
  ↓
Question 3: Installation location?
  ├─ Main floor, AC-controlled → Standard timeline
  ├─ Basement, high MVTR → ADD 5–7 days + mandatory dehumidifier
  └─ Crawlspace → ADD 10+ days (high vapor risk)
  ↓
Question 4: Home baseline RH at delivery?
  ├─ <30% → Shortest timeline (fast drying)
  ├─ 30–50% → Standard timeline
  ├─ 50–65% → Add 5–7 days
  └─ >65% → Add 10+ days; consider dehumidifier
  ↓
Calculate total acclimation days
  ↓
Schedule install date = delivery date + acclimation days + 1–2 day buffer
  ↓
Set RH target = acclimation environment, NOT installation environment
  (This is critical: acclimate for the condition AFTER you leave, not the week of install)
  ↓
MONITOR: Daily meter readings on 5+ samples
  INSTALL when: wood MC within ±0.5% of target, home RH stable
```

## Summary

Toronto's seasonal humidity extremes—from 25% RH in January to 70% RH in August—demand acclimation protocols calibrated to the month, location, and species. Cutting corners saves 5–10 days but costs $5k–15k in callbacks.

**The non-negotiable rule:** Acclimate wood to the RH it will experience _after installation_, not the RH on delivery day. Use moisture meter + RH monitor + daily readings. Document everything. When the customer's floor is perfect 18 months later, they won't remember the 3-week acclimation delay. When it fails because you rushed, they will remember you forever.

**Acclimation is insurance against $10k failures. Sell it that way.**
