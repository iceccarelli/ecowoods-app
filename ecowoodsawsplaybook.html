PART ONEWhy AWS is at the forefront

AWS did not win developer mindshare by having the best marketing site. It won by publishing the reference material its own industry needed and then never stopping. The competitive moat is not the content — it's that the content is produced by mechanisms rather than by campaigns.

That distinction matters more than any individual tactic on this page. A campaign produces ten articles and ends. A mechanism produces an article every week for six years because the process, not a person's enthusiasm, generates it. Amazon's own internal phrasing for this is blunt: good intentions don't work, mechanisms do. Every item below is a mechanism.

Strip away the scale and AWS runs a content ladder with seven rungs, each answering a different question at a different depth. Almost nobody in the trades runs more than two.

PART TWOThe seven mechanisms, transferred

1 · The Well-Architected Framework

AWS's single most consequential publication is not a product. It is a named, versioned, opinionated framework — six pillars, a question set per pillar, and a free tool that scores your workload against it. It converted AWS's opinion into the industry's measuring stick. Competitors now describe their own architectures in AWS's vocabulary.

The mechanism: publish the standard, and you are no longer a participant in the comparison — you are the axis it runs along.

Becomes

The Ecowoods Well-Installed Framework — a versioned public specification for judging a hardwood installation. Six pillars, each with a pass/fail question set: moisture & acclimation, substrate & flatness, fastening or adhesive system, finish system & cure, dust containment & site protection, sequence & handover.

Then the part that makes it a weapon: a self-assessment tool at /framework/assess where a homeowner scores the quote they just received from someone else against it, and gets a PDF report naming what is missing. Every competitor's quote in Toronto becomes an input to an Ecowoods instrument. That is the highest-leverage single build available to this business, and it is entirely mechanism — no advertising spend, no invented claims.

2 · Decision guides

"Choosing an AWS analytics service." A fixed format: the criteria that actually matter → a comparison table → a decision tree → a recommendation with the conditions under which it changes. These rank for the highest-intent queries in existence, because they match the exact shape of the question a buyer is already asking.

Becomes

A /guides/ surface with the same fixed schema. Solid vs engineered · site-finished vs prefinished · oil-modified vs waterborne vs hardwax oil · refinish vs replace · species selection for radiant heat · nail vs glue vs float. Six guides covers essentially every real decision a Toronto homeowner faces. The /design configurator is already half of this — it does the choosing, but publishes none of the reasoning.

3 · The dated feed — "What's New"

Small entries. One canonical URL each. Published relentlessly, with RSS. It is why the industry hears about AWS from AWS rather than from a competitor's comparison page — and why crawlers return daily instead of monthly.

Becomes

/feed.xml — shipped in patch 33, RSS 2.0 over all papers, articles and case studies, derived from the loaders so it cannot drift. The next step is the thing it feeds: a /whats-new surface with a genuinely high cadence — new species in stock, a machine added to the fleet, a finish system qualified, a code or standard update, a seasonal humidity advisory for the GTA. Short entries, dated, cheap to write, compounding.

4 · Published pricing and a calculator

AWS publishes per-unit pricing for every service and gives away a calculator that estimates your bill before you sign anything. In an industry built on opaque quoting, that was a shock — and it became the reason buyers started their research on AWS's own site.

Becomes

Flooring is more opaque than cloud computing was. Nobody publishes prices. A documented, honest cost model at /pricing with a real calculator — square footage, species band, substrate condition, stairs, finish system, removal of existing — producing a range with the assumptions stated. Not a lead-capture form with a number behind it. The actual model, visible.

This is the highest-risk, highest-reward item on the list, and it is entirely Francisco's call — the numbers are his. But note that lib/pricing.ts is currently eight lines, and one of the three published papers already has a section called fixed-price. The intent exists; the surface does not.

5 · Reference architectures

Complete, diagrammed, downloadable solutions to a whole problem — not a component. They are the most-shared artifacts AWS produces because they let someone else look competent in a meeting.

Becomes

Reference installations. "Radiant-heat main floor, 1920s Toronto semi, engineered white oak" as a complete published spec: substrate condition, moisture targets, acclimation schedule, fastening system, finish build, cure timeline, expected seasonal movement — diagrammed, downloadable, reusable. Six of these cover most of the GTA housing stock. They are also, not incidentally, exactly what an architect or designer forwards to a client.

6 · Machine-readability as a first-class product

Every AWS surface has an API, a CLI and an SDK. Nothing is human-only. The consequence in 2026 is that AI agents can operate AWS directly, and a service an agent can use is a service an agent recommends.

Becomes

Ecowoods is already unusually far ahead here — /llms.txt, /ai.txt, a schema.org entity graph with @id cross-references, and now an RSS feed. The next two steps are genuinely frontier for a trade business:

  • A public JSON knowledge API — /api/knowledge returning the full corpus (papers, guides, framework, glossary) as structured JSON under a permissive citation licence.
  • An MCP server at /mcp, so Claude, ChatGPT and any agent can query Ecowoods' knowledge base as a tool rather than scraping a page. Almost no business in any trade has this. It is a small build on top of the manifest architecture that already exists.

7 · Owning the vocabulary

AWS defines its terms, publishes a glossary, and the industry adopts the definitions. Whoever writes the definition that language models quote has already won the question.

Becomes

A canonical glossary at /glossary, one addressable page per term, each with DefinedTerm schema: acclimation, MC differential, cupping, crowning, telegraphing, overwood, lippage, sanding sequence, screen-and-recoat, dry-back time, sub-floor flatness tolerance. When someone asks an assistant "what is cupping in hardwood floors," the answer should be sourced from ecowoods.ca. This is cheap to build, mechanically derived from a manifest exactly like papers.ts, and disproportionately effective for AI citation.

PART FOURThe build order

Sequenced by leverage per unit of effort, not by appeal.

Patch 33 ready

The link graph and the feed. Three orphaned routes wired into the chrome, the 16 city pages linked for real, /feed.xml, and verify-links.mjs — a guard that fails the build on any route with no way in, so the /papers failure cannot recur a fourth time.

34 · Glossary

25–40 terms, manifest-driven exactly like papers.ts, DefinedTerm schema on each, cross-linked from every paper. Pure structure — the definitions come from the papers that already exist.

35 · Decision guides

The /guides surface plus the first two guides in a fixed schema. Highest commercial-intent content type that does not exist yet.

36 · The Framework

/framework — six pillars, versioned, published as a specification with a permanent citation URL. The document, not yet the tool.

37 · The assessment tool

/framework/assess. Scores any contractor's quote against the framework and returns a PDF. The PDF pipeline already exists for invoices and quotes.

38 · MCP server + knowledge API

/mcp and /api/knowledge. Makes the corpus directly queryable by agents rather than scrapeable.

PART FIVEThe two risks that outrank everything above

1 · The case studies may contain claims nobody measured

The five case studies carry precise engineering assertions — "zero adhesion failure and stair deflection <1mm over 18 months", moisture readings to one decimal place, cure-day counts — and one carries a specific residential street address. They were produced during the earlier autonomous agent run.

If those numbers were not measured on those jobs, every item in this playbook is built on sand. An answer engine that catches one fabricated figure discounts the entire domain, and the authority strategy inverts into a liability. The street address is separately a privacy problem regardless of provenance.

This is the one thing to resolve before publishing anything further. Either the readings exist in Francisco's records and stay, or they come out. There is no third option.

2 · Zero verified photographs

This is a hardwood flooring business with no verified photographs of its own completed work. Every mechanism above raises the ceiling on credibility; photographs are the floor. A homeowner deciding on a five-figure job looks at pictures before they read a whitepaper, and an authority strategy with no evidence of craft reads as a content operation rather than a trade.

audit/PHOTO_SHOT_LIST.md already specifies what to capture. This is the highest-value non-code action available and it needs nothing from me.

PART SIXWhat only Francisco can decide

Q1 · Pricing

Do we publish a real cost model with a calculator? It is the strongest differentiator on the list and the one that cannot be walked back. The numbers are yours.

Q2 · The framework's content

I can build the structure, the versioning, the schema and the assessment tool. The six pillars and their pass/fail thresholds are trade judgment — they have to come from you, and they have to be defensible when a competitor attacks them.

Q3 · Case-study provenance

Were those measurements taken? Which of the five describe real Ecowoods jobs, and may the address be published?

Q4 · Publication cadence

A feed with nothing new in it is worse than no feed. What can this business actually sustain — weekly, biweekly, monthly? Answer honestly; the mechanism has to survive a busy season.

Q5 · Naming

"The Ecowoods Well-Installed Framework" is a placeholder. The name matters more than usual, because the goal is for other people to say it.
