# Machine 2 — Floor Edger

**Typical Professional Examples:** Lägler Flip®, Clarke / American Sanders Super 7R, Galaxy RS-100

## Purpose
Reaches every place the belt sander cannot: walls, baseboards, closets, under cabinets, stairs, and tight corners.

## How It Works
- High-speed rotating disc (typically 150–178 mm / 6–7 inches).
- Compact body allows close approach to vertical surfaces.
- Must follow the exact same progressive grit sequence used in the field.
- Most aggressive of the four core machines in terms of material removal per square centimetre.

## Key Educational Points
- Edges and transitions are where most low-quality jobs fail visually.
- Poor technique produces highly visible swirl marks under clear finish.
- Corners and detail areas often still require hand scraping or orbital detail sanding after the edger.
- Dust control is more challenging than on the big machine — HEPA connection is non-negotiable.

## Safety & Skill Notes
Technique is everything.  
The edger can dig in quickly if the operator leans or pauses.  
Always match the grit of the current field pass; never edge ahead of the main sanding sequence.
